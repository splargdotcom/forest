How this forest works

Start with Height genes in the colour menu and press +10 years a few times. Inspect a canopy tree and a shaded tree. Then open Selection to see whose genes reach new seedlings.

Genotype is the inherited allele pair for each trait. In this model, their mean sets the trait value. Phenotype is the tree that actually develops: its height, crown, trunk and roots depend on those genes, age and conditions.

Each seed gets one randomly chosen allele from each parent for every trait. Small mutations can occur in either direction. Resource access affects growth, survival and seed production. Nearby adults supply pollen; isolated trees can self-fertilise. Only germinated seedlings count as established offspring.

The forest has overlapping generations. Death opens space; seeds can wait up to six years. Growth and reproduction are calculated once per model year, at spring; the seasons mark that cycle visually.

Try a comparison. Open Selection and run the 60-year selection experiment: two forests begin from the same seed and settings, with competition as the changed condition. Or use Gene lab for two controlled comparisons: identical genes under different light, and different height genes under an identical environment. When you inspect a tree, Trace lineage highlights its pedigree. You can also click any allele value in the genotype panel to trace that specific copied allele through established descendants. Unchanged copies are separated from branches where a mutation altered the inherited value.

An illustrative model with five additive traits, stylised ecology and accelerated years. Real tree traits involve many genes and more complex inheritance. There is no target genome or guaranteed evolutionary winner.
